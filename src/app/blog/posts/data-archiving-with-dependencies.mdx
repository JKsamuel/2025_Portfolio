---
title: "Archive Data Only After Mapping Its Consumers"
summary: "Moving history out of a warehouse requires understanding every procedure and report that still reads it."
publishedAt: "2026-03-11"
tag: "Data Architecture"
---

Archiving older rows to a lake can reduce pressure on a serving database, but the migration is not simply export and delete. Stored procedures, views, reports, and downstream jobs may still depend on the staging tables.

Before changing retention, inventory those dependencies and identify which objects need recent data, full history, or a point-in-time snapshot. Preserve a replayable export, reconcile row counts and key ranges, then move consumers in stages. Keep the serving contract stable where possible while the storage implementation changes underneath it.

A review of a Bronze-to-ADLS approach showed why dependency mapping matters: the target SQL serving tables could remain useful even when older staging data moved to lake storage. Treat archival as a controlled migration with rollback and validation, not a one-time cleanup task.
